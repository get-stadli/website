The Mariners sold out opening night every year. By the third homestand, a quarter of the seats were empty, and nobody in the front office could say which fans had stopped coming, or why.

## Three systems, no picture of the fan

Tickets lived in one system, emails in another, and the season ticket holders in a spreadsheet the box office kept by hand. A fan who bought four seats in May and two in July looked like two different people.

> We knew our regulars by face at the gate. We didn't know them anywhere else.

### What the team needed

- One record per fan, from the first single-game ticket to the season pass
- Campaigns that go out on their own when a regular misses two games in a row
- Figures the general manager can read on a phone before the gates open

## One season on Stadli

The Mariners moved ticketing and their fan list onto Stadli over one off-season. Stadli AI suggested the first win-back campaign: a short note and a pair of seats in the family section for fans who hadn't been back since June.

By August, the third homestand drew a bigger crowd than opening night.

### What's next

This winter the team is putting group sales on Stadli too, so the birthday parties and the minor hockey teams in the stands get a name, not just a row number.
